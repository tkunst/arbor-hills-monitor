June 16, 2026 surface-methane corrective actions: a close-out tracker

Each of the 75 surface locations EGLE recorded at or above 500 ppm is a separate item GFL must close out. This page tracks what the public record shows for each one.

On June 16, 2026, EGLE's Air Quality Division recorded 75 surface locations at or above 500 ppm methane above background at Arbor Hills, the highest 148,682 ppm at well 502R. (See the inspection page for the photographs and the full record.) Under the federal NESHAP rule (40 CFR 63.1958(d) and 63.1960(c)(4)), each of these is a documented exceedance on a corrective-action clock, not on its own a declared violation. For every location, to stay out of violation GFL must:

  • Record the location and concentration, with GPS to within 4 meters.
  • Correct it (cover repair or vacuum adjustment) and re-monitor below 500 ppm within 10 calendar days of the exceedance, that is, by about June 26, 2026.
  • Re-check the location one month from the exceedance, about July 16, 2026.
  • If a location reaches 500 ppm three times in the quarter, install a new well or collection device within 120 days of the initial exceedance, about October 14, 2026.
  • Document the corrective actions in the Semi-Annual NESHAP report (40 CFR 63.1981(h)).

What the public record shows, as of September 1, 2026. No per-location re-monitoring record (the 10-day re-monitor or the 1-month re-check) appears in the public file for any of the 75 locations. GFL has stated (in its August 21, 2026 letter to EGLE, relayed in EGLE's August 27 letter) that the locations above 500 ppm were successfully remediated, but that letter contains no per-location dates or readings. The First Semi-Annual 2026 NESHAP report, where these corrective actions must be documented under 63.1958(d) and 63.1981(h), has not appeared in EGLE's nSITE record for Arbor Hills as of this date.

The 120-day extension EGLE approved on August 27, 2026 is a separate track (the perimeter-monitor fenceline exceedances, under Consent Judgment 5.5(E)). It does not extend the June 26 surface-methane deadline.

Status below reads open for a location where the public record does not yet show close-out. It is not a finding that GFL failed to act; it flips to documented once a re-monitoring record surfaces.
