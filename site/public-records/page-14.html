2022-10-13 · Arbor Hills Landfill · evidence · urgent

Violation Notice

EGLE issued a Violation Notice to Arbor Hills Landfill on October 13, 2022, for failure to timely identify and correct causes of Perimeter Methane Action Level exceedances. The company exceeded the 40 ppm methane threshold on 34 separate days between June and September 2022, violating Consent Judgment No. 2020-0593-CE paragraph 5.5 E. Despite conducting a root cause analysis that identified a cell liner tie-in problem and a suspected 650-foot band of elevated methane near monitors 4 and 5, the company failed to prevent continued exceedances within the required 48-hour corrective action window.

Key data point: 34 days of Perimeter Methane Action Level exceedances (40 ppm threshold) from June 10 through September 20, 2022; Consent Judgment violation CJ No. 2020-0593-CE paragraph 5.5 E.

2022-09-20 · Arbor Hills Landfill · evidence · notable

On-Site Inspection

EGLE conducted a targeted Surface Emission Monitoring (SEM) survey on September 20, 2022, at Arbor Hills Landfill to investigate recent methane action-level exceedances near Perimeter Monitors 4 and 5. The inspection identified 7 locations with methane concentrations exceeding 500 ppm above background, with the highest readings (up to 23,670 ppm) associated with an old French drain system running parallel to the perimeter road. EGLE recommended that the landfill address all 7 exceedance locations and consider installing a horizontal gas-collection well in the area of concern.

Key data point: 7 monitored methane exceedances ≥500 ppm above background detected September 20, 2022, with peak readings of 23,670 ppm near an old French drain system; Consent Judgment No. 2020-0593-CE action-level threshold is >40 ppm over 15-minute average.

2022-09-19 · Arbor Hills Remediation Area · opinion · notable

NPDES Individual Permit

The Conservancy Initiative submitted comments on NPDES Permit MI0045713 opposing the permit's lack of PFAS (PFOS/PFOA) monitoring requirements for treated groundwater discharged from the Arbor Hills Landfill Remediation Site into Johnson Creek. The group argues that the site's history of widespread PFAS contamination, leachate handling failures, and poor compliance record justify mandatory periodic monitoring as a low-cost protective measure. They cite a 2021 stormwater pond sample exceeding PFOS limits by 33-fold and an August 2022 leachate spill into Johnson Creek, requesting at minimum seasonal monitoring at an estimated cost under $1,000 per occurrence.

Key data point: Stormwater detention pond contained 400 µg/L PFOS (limit 12 µg/L, 33× exceedance); over 80% of 130+ samples in 2021 STSWCS contained PFOS exceeding Michigan surface water standards; August 4, 2022 leachate spill to Johnson Creek (VN-013462).
