2021-09-14 · Arbor Hills Landfill · evidence · notable

On-Site Inspection

EGLE conducted a Full Compliance Evaluation and Partial Compliance Inspection of Arbor Hills Landfill on September 14, 2021, under the Renewable Operating Permit (ROP MI-ROP-N2688-2011a) and federal standards including MACT Subpart AAAA and the Federal Plan (40 CFR Part 62, Subpart OOO). The facility was found in substantial compliance with the ROP and applicable requirements, though EGLE notes ongoing multi-media enforcement negotiations and a lawsuit filed in October 2020 for continued non-compliance with gas collection and control system operation.

Key data point: Surface methane monitoring Q2 2021: 15 exceedances above 500 ppm detected and cleared within the quarter; H2S levels in wells ranged from 0 to 6500 ppm (AHWW257R), with ~16 wells above 1000 ppm; Utility Flare operated 356 of 365 days (Jan–Aug 2021); hydrogen sulfide in LFG to flares averaged 325 ppm (near permit limit of 408 ppm with 80% threshold of 326 ppm).

2021-09-14 · Arbor Hills Landfill · evidence · notable

On-Site Inspection

EGLE conducted a Surface Emission Monitoring (SEM) inspection of Arbor Hills Landfill on September 14, 2021, finding 18 locations with methane concentrations exceeding 500 ppm above background, the federal compliance threshold under 40 CFR Part 62 Subpart OOO and NESHAP. The inspection also identified one leachate outbreak and a small leachate pipe leak. All 18 exceedances triggered mandatory corrective action protocols including re-monitoring within 10 calendar days and potential well installation within 120 days if exceedances persist.

Key data point: 18 locations exceeded 500 ppm methane above background (federal limit); highest reading 15,662 ppm at Penetration WW 432; leachate outbreak at least 100 yards long documented, September 14, 2021.

2021-06-09 · Arbor Hills Remediation Area · procedural · routine

Compliance Communication

EGLE denied Arbor Hills Landfill's request to use compost leachate as a dust suppressant on landfill access roads. The denial was based on Part 115 Solid Waste Management rules, which prohibit disposal of liquid waste unless it meets specific exceptions for household waste, leachate, or gas condensate. The compost residuals in question do not qualify for these exceptions and therefore cannot be used for dust control.

Key data point: Compost leachate dust-suppressant request denied; does not meet leachate definition under Rule 299.4103(s) and violates Rule 430 prohibition on liquid waste disposal.
