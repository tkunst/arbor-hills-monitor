What a healthy vs. a developing SET Event looks like, for these three measures

As a SET (Subsurface Elevated Temperature) Event develops, the wellhead signature for these three measures shifts in a predictable direction, away from healthy anaerobic decomposition. Shaded bands on the charts below mark the same thresholds.

Temperature

●Normal
Below 131°F

Typical operating range for healthy anaerobic decomposition.

↑Rises

Methanogenesis slows once temperatures pass 131°F and is severely curtailed above 160°F. Desirable microbial activity halts near 170°F. At 160°F and above, site infrastructure is also at risk: PVC/HDPE piping, geosynthetic liners, and gas/leachate collection systems can soften or melt.

Oxygen (O₂)

●Normal
Under 2%

Should sit near zero at a healthy wellhead.

↑Rises

Detectable O₂ above baseline signals air intrusion feeding the reaction, the top SET Event trigger.

CH₄:CO₂ Ratio

●Normal
Roughly 1.0 or higher

Methane and carbon dioxide track together in stable landfill gas.

↓Falls

The ratio collapses well below 1.0 as methanogenesis slows while thermal oxidation keeps CO₂ elevated, a clearer signal than either gas alone. Below 0.6 is a warning, below 0.2 is a smoldering signature.

How the clusters are defined. Cluster membership (Eastern Hot Core / Western Warm Field) matches the interactive thermal map: wells that reached 131°F or higher are grouped so that a well joins a cluster when it sits within 400 feet of any other well already in it (single-linkage clustering). At 400 feet, wells that are genuine neighbors group together (the two hottest wells sit about 370 feet apart) while the eastern and western clusters stay distinct. The split is not sensitive to that exact distance: re-run at 100, 200, and 400 feet, using two independent well-coordinate sources, and separately for each year from 2021 to 2026, the same two-cluster result (an eastern hot core and a western warm field) held throughout. Well-picker descriptions are computed from this dataset.
