Perimeter air-monitoring dataset

Fence-line methane and hydrogen-sulfide readings from the six-station perimeter network (MS-1 through MS-6) around the Arbor Hills Landfill, May 2022 to September 2026. Published with a raw snapshot so the enriched table can be rebuilt and checked offline.

This is the ambient, at-the-boundary record behind the monitor's hydrogen sulfide page — the concentrations that actually reach the property line, in parts per billion, as distinct from the far stronger in-gas readings inside the wells. The dataset carries the raw values, so the sensor problems described below are visible in it.

Data-quality note (updated October 6, 2026; corrects the September 25 note): for long stretches, several fence-line stations' hydrogen-sulfide (H2S) channels have reported a single constant value while the station kept reporting and its methane channel kept varying. A sensor in clean air can legitimately read 0.0 for weeks or even months, but a constant non-zero value for months, or a constant 0.0 for more than a year while neighboring stations vary, is a standard data-quality flag, not evidence of clean air. (The feed labels readings below 7 ppb "BDL", and a small number carry no label; all the constant values described here are in that range.) Now: MS-3 and MS-4 are reporting varying readings. MS-5 and MS-2 have resumed varying readings only recently, so their records are short: MS-5 since September 26, 2026 (up to 11.0 ppb), after reading 0.0 from May 28, 2025 (apart from one 0.1 ppb reading) and sending no data from September 16 to 26; MS-2 since September 24, 2026 (up to 2.3 ppb), after reading 0.0 from June 30, 2025. MS-6 read 3.0–3.2 ppb from May 28, 2025 (apart from two readings on June 11, 2026) until September 29, 2026, and since resuming on October 5 has read 0.0 ppb H2S and 0–1 ppm methane every hour. MS-1 has sent no data since noon on September 17, 2026, after reading exactly 0.0 for both gases from 9 p.m. on September 16. Earlier: MS-1 read a constant 3.0 or 3.1 ppb from May 28, 2025 to May 28, 2026, starting the same hour MS-5 and MS-6 went flat; MS-3 read a constant 5.4 ppb from July 15 to October 16, 2025, and 3.1 ppb from November 24 to December 1, 2025, the same value MS-1 and MS-6 read in those hours. A quiet record at a station during these periods is not evidence of clean air. The stations reporting varying readings recorded hourly values above 30 ppb (the 2022 Consent Judgment's fence-line action level, which is defined on a 15-minute rolling average; an hourly value from the public feed is a screening signal, not a confirmed crossing), most recently on September 14, 2026, up to 153.4 ppb at MS-1. None of them has recorded an hourly value at or above 30 ppb since. These are observations from GFL's public perimeter feed, hosted by Barr Engineering; the cause has not been publicly confirmed by GFL, EGLE or Barr Engineering. Status as of October 6, 2026, 11:00 a.m. ET.
