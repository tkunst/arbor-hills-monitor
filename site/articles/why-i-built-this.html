Unanswered Questions Regarding Arbor Hills · Part 1

Why I Built This

Trisha Kunst

I live in Northville Township, Michigan, near the Arbor Hills Landfill in neighboring Salem Township, Washtenaw County. Several facilities regulated by Michigan's Department of Environment, Great Lakes, and Energy (EGLE) sit on and around this one site, including the landfill itself, its groundwater Remediation Area, the Arbor Hills Energy gas-to-electricity plant, and the Emerald RNG gas-processing plant. Each files its own permits, inspection reports, and violation notices with the state, on its own schedule, in its own database.

I am a data scientist. When I started reading this site's regulatory record for myself, I found that the record was public, and also that almost nobody was reading all of it, continuously, across every facility tied to the property. A document that mattered could sit unread in a state database for months. A pattern only becomes visible to someone who is watching for it all the time, not checking in occasionally.

So I built a small program that does the continuous part. Every day it checks Michigan EGLE's public filing systems for every facility tied to this site, downloads anything new, and reads what it can: a routine report, a violation notice, a permit renewal, a measured reading checked against a permitted limit. It keeps a running, dated record of what it finds, and it emails a plain-language summary to a short, curated list of people whose role touches this record.

Why did I build this instead of trusting that someone else would? Because I want to know, from the primary documents themselves, whether the facility near my home is being run the way its permits say it will be. I built this to know if my family is safe without having to move. Not to win an argument. To know.

That gives this project one job: get the primary source right, and say plainly what it shows and what it doesn't yet show. It takes data from any source with a clear paper trail behind it: EGLE, the operator, townships and county records, community groups, whoever has it, as long as it is sourced and I can check it myself. If GFL's own compliance team wanted to send supplemental data, I would take that too, on the same terms.

This is the first entry in a series I am calling "Unanswered Questions Regarding Arbor Hills." The questions in that series are not rhetorical. They are the specific things the public record does not yet answer, stated plainly, alongside what I do and do not know.
